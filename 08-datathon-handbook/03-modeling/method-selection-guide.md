## 🧭 The 3-Step Rule That Holds in Every Scenario

1. **Cheap, hard-to-fool baseline** for the scenario (below).
2. **Validate the way the test set will be built** (group / time / spatial split, see [`cross-validation-guide.md`](cross-validation-guide.md)).
3. **Upgrade by blending diverse model families** on out-of-fold predictions, not by hunting for one best model.

Whatever the matrix says, **the method that wins on your locked OOF folds wins.** Verify with the [Beat-the-Baseline Ladder](ml-toolbox.md).

## Tabular data: size, feature type and the end of "trees always win"

The older evidence favours trees. Grinsztajn et al. report tree-based models still state of the art on medium-sized data (about 10k samples) over deep nets, citing robustness to uninformative features, non-rotation-invariance and irregular target functions ([researchgate, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362123616_Why_do_tree-based_models_still_outperform_deep_learning_on_tabular_data)). TabZilla (19 algorithms, 176 datasets) found the NN-versus-GBDT gap negligible on many datasets, with light GBDT tuning sometimes mattering more than family choice. It also found GBDTs better on larger data and much better on skewed, heavy-tailed features, and found TabPFN v1 best on average but only rank 4.88 ([arXiv 2305.02997, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02997)). These results predate the newer benchmarks, so treat the "trees win" framing as superseded for default advice.

Newer benchmarks shift the picture. TabArena (51 datasets) reports that foundation models excel on smaller datasets, that validation method and ensembling of hyperparameter configurations matter a lot, and that cross-model ensembles advance the state of the art ([arXiv 2506.16791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16791); [repo](https://github.com/autogluon/tabarena)). TALENT (300 datasets) reports that ModernNCA, TabM, RealMLP and small-data foundation models perform similar to or better than GBDTs, with ensembling improving results dramatically ([TALENT](https://github.com/LAMDA-Tabular/TALENT)). TabM claims average rank 1.7 against 15+ models on 46 datasets, ahead of XGBoost, CatBoost and LightGBM, but that is the authors' own benchmark and the summary is secondary ([arXiv 2410.24210](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.24210)). RealMLP, with meta-tuned defaults for both MLPs and GBDTs, is competitive with GBDTs from 1K to 500K samples ([NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/2ee1c87245956e3eaa71aaba5f5753eb-Paper-Conference.pdf)). All of this is SNIPPET-ONLY, and no exact Elo or rank tables were retrieved.

For size, the only quantitative anchors are vendor and author claims. TabPFN-2.5 handles 50,000 rows by 2,000 features, reports a 100% win rate against default XGBoost on classification sets with at most 10,000 rows and 500 features, and 87% up to 100K rows and 2K features. It also carries a quadratic-attention memory cost ([arXiv 2511.08667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08667)). TabICL claims 100K by 500 features on 5 GB of GPU memory and says it beats TabPFNv2 and CatBoost on 53 datasets with more than 10K samples ([arXiv 2502.05564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05564)). The bands in the matrix (foundation models up to ~10k, GBDT plus MLP ensembles from 10k to 100k, GBDT-led stacks above) are **our inference from these anchors, not a published decision rule**. A GPU is effectively required for the foundation models, which matters under hackathon compute budgets. No sourced threshold exists for when ExtraTrees, KNN, SVM or linear models beat GBDTs; a single diabetes study saw LightGBM pull ahead of logistic regression above 10,000 rows ([Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20149-z)), which is one domain.

On feature type, CatBoost is claimed strongest among GBDTs with high-cardinality categoricals, mixed types and moderate-to-high noise (log-loss 0.1377 against LightGBM 0.1636 on Amazon), but that comes from a CatBoost-promoting Substack summary and is UNVERIFIED ([valeman](https://valeman.substack.com/p/where-catboost-beats-xgboost-and); [CatBoost paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09516)). A low-authority aggregator puts logistic regression at 91.2% mean ROC-AUC on numeric data but 77.5% on hybrid data ([aimultiple](https://aimultiple.com/tabular-models)). Whether current XGBoost handles categoricals natively was not verified in the notes.

For real competition behaviour, Kaggle Playground winners blend rather than pick. Chris Deotte's reported first-place April 2025 solution was a three-level stack of 72 models (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, NNs, TabPFN, KNN, SVR, Ridge, Random Forest) on GPU, and XGBoost was the best single model in other wins. The source is a Medium blog the researchers could not open ([Medium, SNIPPET-ONLY](https://medium.com/@gauurab/kaggle-playground-how-top-competitors-actually-win-in-2025-c75d4b380bb5)). Our inference is that using the original pre-synthetic data and GPU stacking are Playground-specific levers that matter less in real, small-data datathons. No main-track Kaggle or datathon winner write-ups were retrieved.

For interpretable models, EBM is reported at AUROC 0.928 against XGBoost 0.927 on Adult Income, with exact editable explanations ([arXiv 2106.09680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.09680); [interpretml](https://github.com/interpretml/interpret); attribution uncertain, one dataset). No multi-dataset benchmark quantifies the accuracy loss. Using EBM or logistic regression when interactions are weak, data are small or judges need explanations is our inference.

## Time series, panel and spatial: simple baselines are the bar, covariates decide the winner

The regime matters. In M4 (100k heterogeneous series), pure ML and NN methods did worse than ARIMA/ETS and statistical combinations; only the ES-RNN hybrid won, with a claimed 9.4% sMAPE gain ([Uber blog](https://www.uber.com/at/en/blog/m4-forecasting-competition/); [M4 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.03279)). In M5 (hierarchical, intermittent retail with prices and events), all top-50 methods were ML and the winner equal-weighted LightGBM models trained at different aggregation levels ([M5 paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169207021001874)). Favorita winners standardised on LightGBM with origin lags, though the supporting write-up is a single low-authority repo ([Kaggle learnings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.07701); [repo](https://github.com/gersonlramos/Store-Sales---Time-Series-Forecasting)). In M6 (finance), only **23.3% of teams beat benchmarks on accuracy and 6.7% on both accuracy and portfolio**, and frequent updating plus forecast combination helped ([M6](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13357)). All are SNIPPET-ONLY.

Pretrained foundation models now lead the aggregate leaderboards. fev-bench (100 tasks) reports Chronos-2, TiRex and TimesFM-2.5 generally dominating statistical and task-specific deep models, with TiRex at 86.7% win rate and TimesFM-2.5 at 82.1% on the full benchmark and Chronos-2 at 90.3% on the covariate subset. The notes themselves flag the two full-benchmark rows as coming from a summary that should be checked against the paper ([fev-bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26468)). Chronos-2 (120M parameters) takes past-only or known-future covariates zero-shot ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15821); [repo](https://github.com/amazon-science/chronos-forecasting)), and TabPFN-TS reportedly ranked first on GIFT-Eval in January 2025 ([repo](https://github.com/PriorLabs/tabpfn-time-series)). These leaderboard claims are largely self-reported and carry pretraining-overlap risk (inference), so treat gaps of a few percent as noise. Zero-shot models degrade on short context; one building-energy study saw accuracy fall at 48 hours or less of hourly context and improve to about 480 steps ([arXiv 2506.00630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00630)), a single task. No authoritative minimum length exists in the notes for N-BEATS, NHITS, PatchTST, TFT, Chronos or TimesFM. The rule of thumb of two to three seasonal cycles for ETS/Theta and a few hundred points per series for global deep models is UNVERIFIED practitioner knowledge. No sourced deep-versus-LightGBM comparison on a shared panel was retrieved, and none by horizon length.

Validation is where most of the sourced guidance sits. Random K-fold is over-optimistic under temporal or spatial dependence; spatial blocks should be at least as wide as the autocorrelation range, and random CV is acceptable only for within-area interpolation ([SDM validation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574954125005308); [GeoAI handbook](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~yhu42/papers/2023_GeoAIHandbook_SpatialCV.pdf); [arXiv 2303.07334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.07334)). Adversarial validation can quantify train/test dissimilarity ([arXiv 2404.12575](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.12575)). The leakage-trap list we would apply is **inference with no retrieved source**: future covariates not truly known at forecast time, scalers or encoders fit on all data, rolling features without a one-step shift, random panel splits, lags shorter than the horizon in direct multi-step setups, and duplicate entities across spatial folds. Always score seasonal naive on the same rolling folds, because it is the normaliser in GIFT-Eval ([repo](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/gift-eval)). For spatial features, the notes contain no head-to-head of KNN-neighbour features against H3 or geohash; those recipes, and GroupKFold on a coarse parent cell as approximate spatial blocking, are UNVERIFIED inference.

Tooling for a 24-48 hour plan is all Apache or BSD-licensed per the fetched READMEs: statsforecast for baselines (its speed claims are vendor claims), mlforecast for global LightGBM with cross-validation, and darts for model breadth ([statsforecast](https://github.com/Nixtla/statsforecast); [mlforecast](https://github.com/Nixtla/mlforecast); [darts](https://github.com/unit8co/darts)). The schedule of baselines first, GBDT second, zero-shot foundation model third, then a blend is our inference, supported only in spirit by M5 and M6 favouring combinations. Release dates, maintenance activity and neuralforecast status were not checked.

## Text, images, anomalies, clusters, recommendations and uplift: cheap defaults, thin evidence

**Text.** Fine-tuned small models (RoBERTa, DeBERTa-v3, Electra) are reported to still significantly outperform zero-shot ChatGPT and Claude in text classification, per the paper's title and abstract only ([arXiv 2406.08660](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08660)). One study shows TF-IDF at 64% F1 against BERT-base at 82.18% ([IIETA](https://www.iieta.org/journals/isi/paper/10.18280/isi.300606)), a single task that should not be generalised. MTEB scores classification with embeddings fed to logistic regression, which supports "frozen embeddings plus linear head" as a default ([BGE docs](https://bge-model.com/tutorial/4_Evaluation/4.2.2.html)). Leaderboard scores quoted by aggregator blogs are UNVERIFIED and change quickly. Temperature 0 with a fixed seed reduces but does not guarantee identical LLM outputs ([arXiv 2407.10457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.10457)), so cache LLM outputs; that recommendation, the fine-tune threshold of about 1-5k labels, and the order TF-IDF, embeddings, fine-tune, LLM are our inference. The notes give no BEIR evidence and no LLM cost or latency numbers.

**Images.** DINOv2 surpasses OpenCLIP and EVA-CLIP in linear evaluation, and its authors report fine-tuning gives only modest gains ([DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07193v2)). One claim that full fine-tuning can worsen results has unconfirmed attribution ([arXiv 2409.16434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16434)). DINOv3 exists as a successor ([arXiv 2508.10104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10104)). The ViT-S/B recipe, augmentation, two-backbone ensembling and LoRA upgrade are inference. Domain-shifted data (medical, satellite, microscopy) is not covered, and no quantified datathon comparison exists.

**Anomaly and fraud.** ADBench (30 algorithms, 57 datasets) found no unsupervised algorithm statistically beats the rest ([arXiv 2206.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426)). A secondary summary says that with 1% labelled anomalies semi-supervised methods (DeepSAD, DevNet, XGBOD) frequently beat the best unsupervised ones (median AUROC reported 71% to 75%), that supervised trees need about 10% labels to match, and that unsupervised methods lose a median 16% AUROC under 6x anomaly duplication ([alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2206.09426)). ECOD's +2% AUROC and +5% AP over the second-best detector is the authors' own evaluation ([arXiv 2201.00382](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00382)); PyOD recommends ECOD and IForest as starting points ([PyOD](https://github.com/yzhao062/pyod/blob/master/README.rst)). A fraud paper reports random splits inflating PR-AUC (0.81 random against 0.553 a month later), likely one dataset ([arXiv 2603.06632](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06632)). No ADBench ranking of IForest against ECOD or COPOD and no autoencoder evidence was retrieved.

**Clustering.** All evidence is blog-grade. KMeans suits roughly spherical clusters and is outlier-sensitive; HDBSCAN needs no k and labels noise; silhouette alone is unreliable, and excluding HDBSCAN noise points can inflate it ([Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/advanced-customer-segmentation-a-comprehensive-comparison-of-hdbscan-dbscan-and-k-means-ce3bcaa7f1a2?gi=54fd4a2a82ab); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387934161_Comparison_of_K-Means_and_HDBSCAN_Clustering_Approaches_to_Enhance_Marketing_Strategies)). Bootstrap stability with adjusted Rand index, profiling on unused features and PCA or UMAP before HDBSCAN on embeddings are our inference. No peer-reviewed KMeans/GMM/HDBSCAN stability benchmark was found.

**Recommendation.** The only numbers are a single hobby repo (ALS Recall@10 0.1182 against item-item 0.0624, but coverage 0.1056 against 0.4836) ([repo](https://github.com/kirtis111/e-commerce-recommendation-system)) and a 2021-22 paper saying properly tuned iALS is competitive ([iALS revisited](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355698565_Revisiting_the_Performance_of_iALS_on_Item_Recommendation_Benchmarks)). Replication studies report large drops (-51% to -72%) from protocol details, with attribution unconfirmed ([arXiv 2501.10143](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10143)). The `implicit` library covers ALS, BPR and LMF ([GitHub](https://github.com/benfred/implicit)). Treat this row as weakly supported; the notes hold no 2023-26 benchmark and nothing on LLM-based recommenders.

**Causal and uplift.** The principle is sourced: response models target "sure things", individual uplift is unobservable so accuracy metrics do not apply, and Qini or cumulative gain curves are the evaluation ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0020025519312022); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uplift_modelling)). The method ranking is not settled. One large Criteo v2.1 study reports causal forest Qini 0.0877, X-learner 0.0778 and T-learner 0.0712 (2.8M held-out rows) with DR indistinguishable from T ([arXiv 2604.06123 listing](https://awesomepapers.io/federated-learning/papers/2604.06123)), while another summary has a plain response LightGBM scoring highest on conversion with causal forest close and T and X trailing ([GitHub](https://github.com/omvyas77/uplift)). **These conflict and both are secondary, so our T-learner-first, causal-forest-upgrade ordering rests on simplicity and small-data noise arguments (inference), not on benchmark evidence.** Confounding in observational data and the need for propensity-overlap checks are general causal-inference reasoning with no retrieved source. CausalML and EconML version and date details are UNVERIFIED ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/causalml/)).

## Cross-cutting choices: threshold before SMOTE, calibrate before pricing, trust CV over the leaderboard

**Imbalance.** The best-sourced finding is against resampling. Across 73 datasets, a strong consistent classifier generally does best and balancing helps only when oversampler hyperparameters are excellent a priori ([arXiv 2201.08528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08528)). In logistic-regression simulations, under/oversampling and SMOTE gave no noticeable discrimination benefit, worsened calibration, strongly overestimated probabilities, and often could not be repaired by recalibration, while shifting the threshold had a similar effect on sensitivity and specificity ([PMC9382395](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9382395/)); a follow-up covers other ML models ([PMC11771573](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11771573/)). All are SNIPPET-ONLY. Our inference is to train plain, pick the threshold on out-of-fold predictions, and treat class weights like resampling for calibration (they shift probabilities upward), which no retrieved source tests directly. Nothing was found comparing focal loss with weights or thresholds on tabular GBDTs, and no AUC effect sizes were retrieved.

**Metrics and calibration.** PR curves are argued to reflect future performance better than ROC under imbalance ([Saito and Rehmsmeier](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0118432)), but counter-papers argue ROC is accurate ([Patterns 2024](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(24)00109-0)); their findings were not read, so the choice stays contested. Platt scaling beats isotonic below roughly 200-1000 calibration cases and isotonic matches or wins at 1000+ ([Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~alexn/papers/calibration.icml05.crc.rev3.pdf)). Decision-curve net benefit puts harms and benefits on one scale ([Vickers and Elkin](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0272989X06295361)). Conformal prediction gives finite-sample coverage sets ([Angelopoulos and Bates](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021arXiv210707511A/abstract)); our inference is that it does not replace calibrated probabilities for expected-value arithmetic. Check reliability curves and Brier score on OOF data before multiplying p by a dollar amount. F-beta guidance and temperature scaling were not verified.

**Ensembling and stacking.** AutoGluon-Tabular uses multi-layer stacking with skip connections and repeated k-fold bagging on OOF predictions, reporting up to 50% relative error reduction on its OpenML benchmark, which is the authors' own claim ([arXiv 2003.06505](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06505)). TabArena and TALENT both report large ensembling gains. With few rows, a constrained weighted or rank average of a handful of diverse models is lower risk than a flexible meta-learner (inference); no quantitative evidence on blend overfitting, rank averaging against stacking, or seed and fold counts was found.

**Validation.** Using one CV loop to both tune and report is optimistically biased and nested CV is the remedy ([Cawley and Talbot](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf)), though a counterview calls nested CV overzealous for most practical selection ([arXiv 1809.09446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.09446)). "Trust your CV over the public leaderboard" is Kaggle norm from a Medium handbook ([shake-up handbook](https://medium.com/global-maksimum-data-information-technologies/kaggle-handbook-fundamentals-to-survive-a-kaggle-shake-up-3dec0c085bc8)). Split to mimic the test (time, group, spatial) in every scenario above.

**Feature engineering versus model choice.** The notes hold no source quantifying feature engineering's share. The only related evidence is TabZilla's finding that tuning can matter more than family and the Playground reports of thousands of engineered features, a blog opinion. Out-of-fold target encoding is practitioner knowledge, UNVERIFIED here.

**Explainability.** Shapley explanations are criticised as general feature-importance measures and may not suit human-centric goals ([Kumar et al., ICML 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/kumar20e.html)). TreeExplainer's default path-dependent mode can give later-split features larger contributions under correlation ([SHAP docs](https://shap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/shap.TreeExplainer.html)), and permutation importance understates correlated twins, so cluster correlated features first ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/permutation_importance.html)). Present SHAP as a model explanation, not a causal effect. What datathon judges accept is not in the notes and is likely rubric-dependent.

## Evidence gaps

The largest gap is access: arxiv.org and medium.com were blocked, so essentially nothing was verified against a full paper, and every figure above should be treated as snippet-level. Specific missing items follow.

- **Tabular:** no TabArena or TALENT rank or Elo tables; no EBM, KNN, SVM, ExtraTrees, FT-Transformer or ResNet numbers; no sourced size threshold for linear or ExtraTrees versus GBDT; no noise-level study beyond a weak CatBoost claim; no datathon or non-Playground winner write-ups; native XGBoost categorical support unverified.
- **Time series and spatial:** exact M4/M5 scores and current GIFT-Eval or fev-bench tables; minimum history for any deep or foundation model; deep versus LightGBM comparison on a common panel; embargo or purging guidance; any H3, geohash or KNN-feature benchmark or geospatial Kaggle write-up; library release and maintenance dates, and neuralforecast status.
- **Text, image, clustering, recommendation:** no BEIR or LLM pricing evidence; accuracy margins from the text benchmark papers; domain-shift image cases; any peer-reviewed clustering benchmark; any recent recommendation benchmark or LLM-recsys evidence.
- **Anomaly and uplift:** no IForest-versus-ECOD ranking or autoencoder evidence; uplift benchmarks are secondary, inconsistent and unconfirmed, and nothing sourced covers pitfalls in non-randomised data.
- **Cross-cutting:** no focal-loss, F-beta or effect-size evidence on imbalance; AUROC-versus-AUPRC counter-papers unread; no blend-overfitting, repeated-CV or adversarial-validation evidence; nothing quantifying feature engineering; nothing on what judges accept as explainability.

## Conclusion

The practical shift is that model-family loyalty has become the weaker lever. On tabular data the benchmarks agree on only two things: ensembling across families helps, and the right validation scheme matters as much as the algorithm. For time series the decisive variable is whether known-future covariates and many series exist, and in every regime a seasonal-naive or popularity baseline should be scored first, because the M4 and M6 history says many sophisticated entries lose to it.

The honest limit of this report is that the decision matrix is a synthesis. Most "first method" cells are inference built on snippet-level, often self-reported evidence, and the size thresholds, the clustering and recommendation rows, and the uplift ranking are the weakest. A team should treat the matrix as an ordered experiment plan to be settled by its own out-of-fold, test-mimicking validation, not as settled ground truth.
