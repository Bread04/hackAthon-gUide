# Datathon rules: dataset licences, external data, leaderboard rules and submission limits

Source access note (2026-10-02): kaggle.com, creativecommons.org, amstat.org, citadel.com, datafest.psu.edu, data-analytics.osu.edu and theregister.com were all blocked by the sandbox egress proxy. The Kaggle rules text below therefore comes from verbatim copies of official Kaggle rules pages that participants committed to public GitHub repos. Two copies were read in full:
- NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Reasoning Challenge rules, 2026: https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt
- Kaggle Playground Series S6 rules, 2026: https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md

They match each other almost word for word, which is good evidence that they are the current standard Kaggle template. ## Q1. What do Kaggle, WiDS, ASA DataFest, Citadel/Correlation One and university datathons say about external data, data use, teams, submissions, leaderboards, code sharing and winner obligations?

### Takeaway
Kaggle uses one standard template. It allows only one account per person and bans private sharing of code or data outside your team. Teams can merge until the merger deadline, but only if the combined submission count fits the cap. Common settings are 5 submissions a day and 2 final selections, and only the Private Leaderboard decides the ranking. External data is allowed by default if it is "reasonably accessible to all" at "minimal cost". Winners must hand over reproducible training and inference code and license it under the competition's winner licence (CC BY 4.0, MIT, Apache 2.0 or None). In-person datathons such as DataFest and Citadel/Correlation One work differently: data is under an NDA and must be deleted after the event, or only public data may be used, and judging is by report or presentation, not a leaderboard.

### Cited Findings

**Kaggle standard rules template (2026 copies)**
- One account. Preamble: "You cannot sign up to Kaggle from multiple accounts and therefore you cannot enter or submit from multiple accounts." §3.5a: "You will be disqualified if you make Submissions through more than one Kaggle account, or attempt to falsify an account to act as your proxy." — [Nemotron rules copy](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Teams. "you may join or form only one Team. Each Team member must be a single individual with a separate Kaggle account." The maximum team size is set per competition: 5 in the Nemotron challenge and 3 in Playground S6. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt); [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md)
- Team merging, from the competition-specific rules: "the combined Team must have a total Submission count less than or equal to the maximum allowed as of the Team Merger Deadline. The maximum allowed is the number of Submissions per day multiplied by the number of days the competition has been running." The general rules §3.5c add that a merger must (i) not exceed the maximum team size, (ii) not exceed the submissions allowed for one team, (iii) be completed before the earlier of the merger deadline or the competition deadline, and (iv) meet all other rules. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Submission limits in both 2026 copies: "You may submit a maximum of five (5) Submissions per day" and "You may select up to two (2) Final Submissions for judging." — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt); [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md). Limits vary by competition. One team's notes record a live Kaggle limit of 1 submission a day, with 2 final selections, in a 2026 agent competition — [dalloliogm/kaggle_competitions AGENTS.md](https://github.com/dalloliogm/kaggle_competitions/blob/HEAD/competitions/autonomous-agent-prediction-beta/AGENTS.md) (SNIPPET-ONLY, from GitHub code search). WiDS 2020 allowed 10 a day — (SNIPPET-ONLY, WebSearch summary of https://www.kaggle.com/c/widsdatathon2020/rules).
- Final selection default. A "Final Submission" is "the Submission selected by the user, or automatically selected by Kaggle in the event not selected by the user". — [Nemotron definitions §3.18c](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Public vs private leaderboard: "The potential winner(s) are determined solely by the leaderboard ranking on the Private Leaderboard." The Public Leaderboard is "a ranked display of Participants' Submission scores against a representative sample of the test data". The Competition Data "will contain private and public test sets. Which data belongs to which set will not be made available to Participants." On ties, "the Submission that was entered first to the Competition will be the winner". — [Nemotron §3.7, §3.18](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Simulation competitions can override the boilerplate. In Kaggle's 2026 "kaggriculture" bot competition, the evaluation page said "only the latest 2 submissions are tracked... also used for final leaderboard evaluation." That contradicts the boilerplate "select up to two". The final ranking came from a Bradley-Terry tournament run after the deadline. — [romansvet/kaggriculture rules notes](https://github.com/romansvet/kaggriculture/blob/HEAD/docs/strategy/2026-09-26-rules1.md), a participant's notes quoting official pages.
- Hand-labelling ban, §3.4b: "Except as expressly allowed in Hackathons... submissions may not use or incorporate information from hand labeling or human prediction of the validation dataset or test data records." — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Private sharing, §3.5d: "No private sharing outside of Teams. Privately sharing code or data outside of Teams is not permitted. It's okay to share code if made available to all Participants on the forums." Under §3.6a, private sharing of "Competition Code", including between separate teams unless they merge, "is a breach of these Competition Rules and may result in disqualification." — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Public code sharing, §3.6b: code may be shared publicly, but only "on Kaggle.com on the discussion forum or notebooks associated specifically with the Competition". By sharing it "you are deemed to have licensed the shared code under an Open Source Initiative-approved license... that in no event limits commercial use." — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Open-source dependencies, §3.6c: unless the specific rules say otherwise, any open-source code in the model must be under an OSI-approved licence "that in no event limits commercial use". In practice this rules out GPL-style or non-commercial code licences unless the host allows them. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- External data and models, §2.6a/b: External Data must be "publicly available and equally accessible to use by all Participants... at no cost to the other Participants, or satisfies the Reasonableness criteria." "The use of external data and models is acceptable unless specifically prohibited by the Host." Their use must be "reasonably accessible to all" and of "minimal cost". Example given: "a small subscription charge to use additional elements of a large language model such as Gemini Advanced are acceptable... Purchasing a license to use a proprietary dataset that exceeds the cost of a prize in the competition would not be considered reasonable." — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt); [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md)
- AutoML is allowed, §2.6c, "provided that the Participant or Team ensures that they have an appropriate license to the AMLT". — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Data security clause (both copies): "You agree not to transmit, duplicate, publish, redistribute or otherwise provide or make available the Competition Data to any party not participating in the Competition." This applies even when the data is CC BY 4.0. — [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md)
- The data-use tiers are host-selected checkboxes. Playground S6 reads "Competition Use and Commercial are checked: You may access and use the Competition Data for any purpose, whether commercial or non-commercial…" — [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md). Other competitions use "Non-Commercial" (forums, academic research and education only) or "Competition Use only" (only for participating in the competition and on Kaggle forums) — (SNIPPET-ONLY: WebSearch summary of Kaggle rules pages such as https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/llm-classification-finetuning/rules).
- The winner licence type varies. It is "CC BY 4.0" for Nemotron 2026, "None" for Playground S6, Apache 2.0 for the Gen AI Intensive Capstone 2025Q1 and MIT for Biohub Cell Tracking. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt); [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md); Apache and MIT are SNIPPET-ONLY from WebSearch of https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/gen-ai-intensive-course-capstone-2025q1/rules and https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/biohub-cell-tracking-during-development/rules
- Winner licence carve-outs: you need not open-source "generally commercially available software" the sponsor can procure "without undue expense". "In the event that input data or pre-trained models with an incompatible license are used… you do not need to grant an open source license… for that data and/or model(s)." — [Nemotron §2.5](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Winner obligations, §2.8: deliver "the final model's software code as used to generate the winning Submission… should include training code, inference code, and a description of the required computational environment". Also provide a reproducible write-up (architecture, preprocessing, loss, hyper-parameters, and a repo link), grant the licence, and sign acceptance and tax documents. Nemotron additionally required a public notebook and write-up to be prize-eligible. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Deadlines after winning: respond to the winner notification within 1 week. Return prize documents within 2 weeks or forfeit. Non-compliant code (for example licence conflicts) may be disqualified, or the winner may be required to fix it within 1 week. Team prize money is split evenly unless the team unanimously agrees otherwise. — [Nemotron §3.8–3.9](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)

**ASA DataFest**

**Citadel / Citadel Securities Datathons (Correlation One)**
- Participants "will utilize only publicly-available data sources and, if applicable, information provided by Citadel or Citadel Securities". They must not use "material, non-public information or confidential or proprietary information… belonging to any third party, including any current or prior employers" — (SNIPPET-ONLY: https://www.citadel.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Citadel-High-School-Terminals-Programs-Certification-and-Terms.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rules checklist for a Kaggle-style datathon. Read the competition-specific section, because it overrides the general rules. Note the team cap, merger deadline, daily cap, number of final selections, data-use tier, winner licence and whether external data is allowed. Never use a second account. Share code only in public competition forums or notebooks. Do not hand-label test data. Select your final submissions yourself, because Kaggle's automatic pick is a fallback. Use only OSI-licensed, commercial-friendly dependencies. Keep a reproducible training and inference repo from day 1.
- A team merge late in the competition can be blocked if both teams have used most of their daily submissions. Plan merges early.
- For NDA-style datathons (DataFest, sponsored university events), assume the data cannot be put in a public GitHub repo and must be deleted afterwards. Portfolio repos should hold code only, never data.

## Q2. Common dataset licences in competitions (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC, CC BY-SA, ODbL, custom DUA): what does each allow a team to do with the data and the solution?

### Takeaway
CC BY 4.0 allows any use, including commercial, if you give attribution. Kaggle's rules can still forbid redistributing the data during the competition. CC BY-NC bars use "primarily intended for… commercial advantage or monetary compensation". CC BY-SA and ODbL are share-alike: a public derived dataset must use the same licence. Under ODbL, publishing a model or chart (a "Produced Work") does not trigger share-alike, but it does need a notice. A custom DUA or NDA (DataFest style) usually forbids redistribution and requires deletion. The licence on the data is separate from the winner licence on the code.

### Cited Findings
- CC BY-NC 4.0 definition: "NonCommercial means not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation." Swapping files peer to peer is NonCommercial if no money changes hands. — [SPDX CC-BY-NC-4.0 text](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-NC-4.0.txt)
- CC BY 4.0 is the full legal code (attribution only), and CC BY-SA 4.0 adds share-alike for Adapted Material. — [SPDX CC-BY-4.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-4.0.txt); [SPDX CC-BY-SA-4.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt). The CC licence deed pages were blocked, so the summary of these deeds comes from the legal code, not the deed.
- Data under CC BY 4.0 inside Kaggle. For Nemotron, "any use of the Data must include an acknowledgement and attribution to the NVIDIA Research team". The Kaggle data-security clause separately bars giving the data to non-participants. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- ODbL 1.0 §4.3: creating and using a Produced Work needs no database notice. "However, if you Publicly Use a Produced Work, You must include a notice" such as "Contains information from DATABASE NAME, which is made available here under the Open Database License (ODbL)." — [SPDX ODbL-1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- ODbL §4.4 share-alike: "Any Derivative Database that You Publicly Use must be only under the terms of: i. This License; ii. A later version…; or iii. A compatible license." Extracting a substantial part into a new database creates a Derivative Database. — [SPDX ODbL-1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- ODbL §4.5: using the database "to create a Produced Work does not create a Derivative Database", and "Use of a Derivative Database internally within an organisation is not to the public". §4.6: if you publicly use a Derivative Database, or a Produced Work made from one, you must offer the derivative database, or a file of the alterations, in machine-readable form. — [SPDX ODbL-1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- Custom DUA or NDA (DataFest): sign the NDA, keep the data secure, delete it at the end, and do not name the data source until all events are done — (SNIPPET-ONLY, see the Q1 DataFest sources).
- The data licence and the solution licence are separate. Kaggle's "WINNER LICENSE TYPE" (for example CC BY 4.0 or None) covers your code. "DATA ACCESS AND USE" covers the provided data. Under §2.5, incompatible-licence data or models used in a winning solution are exempt from the open-source grant. — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt); [Playground S6](https://github.com/leechanwoo-kor/kaggle-playground-series-s6e3/blob/HEAD/docs/rules.md)

### Inferences
- What a team may do with each licence:
  - **CC BY**: publish code, models and write-ups, and reuse the data commercially later, with credit. During a Kaggle competition, still do not repost the raw data elsewhere.
  - **CC BY-NC**: portfolio and academic use are fine. Do not build a startup product on the data.
  - **CC BY-SA**: a published cleaned or augmented dataset must also be CC BY-SA.
  - **ODbL**: same as CC BY-SA for a published cleaned dataset (ODbL or compatible). A published model output or dashboard needs the ODbL notice. If the output was built from a modified database, you must also offer that database or the diff.
  - **DUA/NDA**: code only, no data, and delete the data afterwards.
- Pretrained models used under Kaggle rules must meet the "reasonably accessible" test. Using a non-commercially licensed model may conflict with §3.6c (open-source code must not limit commercial use) unless the host allows it. This is an inference; check each competition's forum clarifications.

## Q3. Examples of disqualifications for rule breaches (private sharing, multiple accounts, hand-labelling test data)

### Takeaway
The best-documented case is the PetFinder.my Adoption Prediction competition (2019, announced January 2020). The 1st-place team "Bestpetting" hid scraped test labels inside an external dataset. They were disqualified, and the key member, Kaggle Grandmaster Pavel Pleskov, was permanently banned. The rules themselves say multi-account use and private sharing lead to disqualification and possible removal from the leaderboard, with loss of points and medals. Beyond PetFinder, documented named cases of multi-account or private-sharing removals were not found in accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
- PetFinder.my. 1st-place team Bestpetting "crawled the pet adoption site to collect the private leaderboard answers, and hid this data in their submission". The prize was $10,000 of a $25,000 pool. They hid about 3,500 scraped samples inside an external dataset ("Cute Cats and Dogs from Pixabay.com") and swapped in leaked labels for only about 1 in 10 pets to avoid suspicion. The cheating was discovered when a later participant got access to the winning code to productionise it. — (SNIPPET-ONLY: [Medium/TDS](https://medium.com/data-science/kaggle-1st-place-winner-cheated-10-000-prize-declared-irrecoverable-bb7e1b639365); [needleinthehay.de](https://needleinthehay.de/blog/2020-01-23-cheating-in-kaggel-competition/); [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/kaggle-data-science-community-rocked-by-pet-adoption-contest-cheating-scandal/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2020/01/21/ai_kaggle_contest_cheat/))
- Outcome: Pleskov was "banned permanently from Kaggle, as 'evidence points towards him being the key party'". He apologised and said he would return the prize money. The team was removed from the leaderboard, so the others moved up one place. One article reports the prize as declared "irrecoverable" — (SNIPPET-ONLY: [hyper.ai](https://hyper.ai/en/news/10449); [Medium/TDS](https://medium.com/data-science/kaggle-1st-place-winner-cheated-10-000-prize-declared-irrecoverable-bb7e1b639365))
- Sanction text in the rules. Under §3.8d the Sponsor can disqualify for "cheating, deception, or other unfair playing practices". §3.8e: "A disqualified Participant may be removed from the Competition leaderboard… Kaggle points or medals, may also not be awarded." The rules also void submissions "obtained through fraud". — [Nemotron](https://github.com/ahb-sjsu/agi-hpc/blob/HEAD/benchmarks/nemotron-rules.txt)
- Mass account suspensions and gaming of kernel or discussion medals (fake upvote accounts) have been reported in Kaggle forum threads — (SNIPPET-ONLY, UNVERIFIED details: https://www.kaggle.com/discussions/product-feedback/299894; https://www.kaggle.com/general/57642)

### Inferences
- The PetFinder case touches several rules at once: undisclosed external data that was not equally accessible, leakage of test labels, and fraud. Datathon teams should declare every external dataset publicly in the forum. Hosts can inspect winning code afterwards.
- Code competitions, which hide the test features and limit runtime, are Kaggle's structural defence against hand-labelling (SNIPPET-ONLY: WebSearch summary).

### Gaps
- Found no accessible, primary, named case of disqualification specifically for multiple accounts or private sharing (Kaggle forum and announcement pages were blocked). Any statement that this is "common" is UNVERIFIED.
- Found no documented DataFest or Citadel disqualification cases.
